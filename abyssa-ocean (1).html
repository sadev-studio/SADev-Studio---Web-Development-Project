From Light To Darkness

The Five Ocean Zones

The deeper you go, the stranger life becomes — pressure builds, light vanishes, and evolution gets creative.

010 – 200m

Sunlight Zone

Where photosynthesis powers coral reefs, kelp forests, and nearly all ocean life we recognize — dolphins, turtles, and reef fish call this home.

02200 – 1,000m

Twilight Zone

Sunlight fades to near-darkness. Bioluminescent creatures begin to outnumber those that rely on sight alone.

031,000 – 4,000m

Midnight Zone

Total darkness, near-freezing water, crushing pressure — home to anglerfish, giant squid, and creatures rarely seen alive.

044,000 – 6,000m

Abyssal Zone

Covers over 75% of the ocean floor. Life here survives on nutrients drifting down from thousands of meters above.

056,000m+

Hadal Zone

Found only in ocean trenches. The Mariana Trench plunges to nearly 11,000m — deeper than Everest is tall.

Icons Of The Deep

Famous Marine Life

The species that define our idea of the ocean — from the largest animal ever to exist, to hunters shaped by 400 million years of evolution.

Largest Animal

Blue Whale

Balaenoptera musculus

Up to 30 meters long — the largest animal to have ever lived on Earth, feeding almost entirely on tiny krill.

Apex Predator

Great White Shark

Carcharodon carcharias

A perfected predator, virtually unchanged for millions of years, capable of sensing a single drop of blood from far away.

Most Social

Bottlenose Dolphin

Tursiops truncatus

One of the few animals known to recognize itself in a mirror, and to call other dolphins by unique names.

Ocean's Wolf

Orca

Orcinus orca

Technically a dolphin, orcas hunt in coordinated family pods with hunting techniques passed down through generations.

Master of Camouflage

Common Octopus

Octopus vulgaris

Has three hearts, blue blood, and can change both color and texture in under a second to vanish into its surroundings.

Ancient Wanderer

Green Sea Turtle

Chelonia mydas

Can live over 80 years and migrates thousands of kilometers back to the exact beach where it hatched, to nest.

Gentle Giant

Giant Manta Ray

Mobula birostris

Wingspans reach up to 7 meters, filter-feeding on plankton while gliding through open water like an underwater bird.

99% Water

Moon Jellyfish

Aurelia aurita

Has no brain, no heart, and no bones — yet has drifted through every ocean on Earth for over 500 million years.

Rainforests of the Sea

Coral Reefs

"Coral reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor, yet support nearly a quarter of all known marine species."

A single reef is a living structure, built over thousands of years by tiny animals called polyps — and it's one of the most fragile ecosystems on the planet.

Home to over 4,000 species of fish, including clownfish, parrotfish, and butterflyfish.

Protect coastlines from storms and erosion, absorbing up to 97% of a wave's energy.

Rising ocean temperatures have already bleached roughly half the world's coral reefs.

The Ocean Needs Us

Threats Facing Our Ocean

◆

Plastic Pollution

An estimated 8 million tons of plastic enter the ocean every year, harming marine life at every level of the food chain.

◈

Coral Bleaching

Rising sea temperatures stress corals until they expel their color-giving algae, often leading to reef death.

◇

Overfishing

Roughly a third of global fish stocks are fished at unsustainable levels, disrupting entire marine ecosystems.

✧

Ocean Acidification

As oceans absorb excess CO₂, the water becomes more acidic — making it harder for shellfish and coral to build their skeletons.
